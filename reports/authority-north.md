# Political authority in the northern part of the sheet, 1500–1700

This checklist covers the Massachusetts Bay coast, central uplands and middle Connecticut valley, lower Merrimack, New Hampshire, the Maine coast and the northern river valleys. Each entry in `js/authority-north.js` carries its own source. The intervals describe the period supported by the record, and the end of evidence can precede the end of a community.

| Area | 1500 to early contact | Local administration and rights through 1665 | War, return and the 1700 endpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shawmut, Mystic and Essex coast | Long habitation on Shawmut. Circa 1600 the tribal history locates Nanapashamet’s network from the Charles toward the Piscataqua. Agawam’s approximate reach is bounded by the Merrimack, North River, Atlantic and Cochichawick. | Squaw Sachem and her sons succeeded Nanapashamet in 1619. At the Mystic Lakes the 1639 deed retained land and uses west of the ponds during her lifetime. Naumkeag acquired an English settlement in 1626 and court in 1635. Boston began on Shawmut in 1630. Ipswich incorporated in 1634. Masconomet’s 1638 agreement described his rights, including Chebacco. | Wampatuck’s successors continued to govern from Ponkapoag. The 1670 agreement with Metacom addressed a disputed boundary whose route has not been recovered here. The 1684–86 Marblehead and Salem deeds identify Wenepoykin’s family. Charles Chickataubut signed the 1695 deed concerning Boston and its islands. The Boston government changed in 1686, 1689 and 1692. |
| Nipmuc interior, mission towns and Nashua valley | Regional Nipmuc occupation of the river and pond country. A year-specific sixteenth-century partition is unresolved. Nashaway occupied the confluence and Waushacum ponds. | Sholan’s land agreement and a trading house date to c. 1643. Lancaster obtained town powers in 1653. Noyes surveyed its bounds in 1659. Natick’s Indigenous town dates to 1651, Hassanamesit’s mission to 1654 and Ponkapoag’s plantation to 1657. | Lancaster’s survey received final consent in October 1672. War disrupted government at Lancaster, forced people from Natick and depopulated Hassanamesit. Natick survivors returned. Lancaster families reoccupied the old settlement by 1681. Hassanamesit families returned by 1698. No late reservation tract has been projected backwards. |
| Middle Connecticut and Deerfield rivers | Nonotuck, Agawam, Pocumtuck, Woronoco and Squakheag are settlements or local political communities in a connected valley. The names do not establish an enclosing boundary at 1500. | The 1636 Springfield deed excepted planted ground. The 1653 Nonotuck deed and English settlement of Northampton in 1654 are separate events. In early 1665 the destruction of the Pocumtuck fort displaced its people. | The 1667 deed retained hunting, fishing and gathering. English settlements at Deerfield and Squakheag were abandoned in 1675. Pocumtuck replanted near Deerfield in 1676. English Deerfield resumed in 1682. Squakheag underwent a second occupation and withdrawal. The valley’s displaced Indigenous communities continued traveling between Schaghticoke, the Connecticut and Canada. |
| Merrimack, Piscataqua and New Hampshire | Archaeology identifies village and seasonal land use. Western Abenaki communities were linked across the region. Place and band names alone cannot establish eleven neighboring political polygons. | Pennacook communities at Wamesit and Pawtucket Falls continued to use the lower Merrimack alongside expanding towns. Dover dates to 1623. Exeter and Hampton date to 1638. Dover entered Massachusetts jurisdiction in 1641, Exeter in 1643. Hampton already had Massachusetts authorization. | War and displacement disrupted the lower Merrimack. A royal commission in 1679 preceded operation of a separate province in 1680. Changes in 1686, 1689, 1690 and 1692 affected English town government. The 1693 New Castle charter divided the Portsmouth jurisdiction at Great Island and on the mainland. |
| Maine coast and eastern edge | Wabanaki communities occupied river mouths. The Penobscot Nation’s chronology gives an early reference to Bessabez in 1568. There is no regional perimeter for his authority. Champlain’s 1605 plan identifies Chouacoit on the Saco. | The temporary Popham fort coexisted with Wabanaki authority. Agamenticus had a Gorges government, then joined a three-town compact in 1649. York and Kittery submitted in 1652. Wells, Cape Porpoise and Saco followed in 1653, other eastern towns in 1658. The royal commissioners took possession at York on July 3, 1665 Gregorian. | Massachusetts commissioners enforced resumption at York in July 1668. Gorges’s proprietary title changed hands in 1678. Danforth, Dominion and Massachusetts provincial governments followed. The Casco agreement in 1678 recognized Wabanaki bargaining power. Renewed war disrupted settlement by 1690. Garrisons survived at the southern towns. The 1699 negotiations supply no surveyed line of occupation. |
| Northern inland edge | Archaeology identifies long habitation in the upper Connecticut and Champlain drainage. Its political divisions cannot be dated parcel by parcel. | The record locates Sokoki and Cowasuck along the Connecticut and other Western Abenaki communities west of the divide. Wartime alliances in the 1650s and 1660s altered settlement patterns. | Refugees traveled north in the 1670s. Alliances with the French in the next war coexisted with independent Western Abenaki governance. No map establishes an exclusive jurisdiction across the uplands in 1700. |

## Boundary construction and registration

* **Agawam.** The earlier eleven-vertex Ipswich sachemship outline is retained for the early seventeenth-century reconstruction. The river and coastal anchors come from the Massachusetts Historical Commission. The line between them remains approximate. The closed outline ends with the 1638 deed as an evidence cutoff. A point documents Masconomet’s continuing authority. This decision leaves his successors’ boundaries open. [Ipswich reconnaissance survey](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/mhc/preservation/survey/town-reports/archives/ips.pdf) and [Ipswich town records](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3d/The_ancient_records_of_the_town_of_Ipswich-_vol._1-_from_1634_to_1650_%28IA_ancientrecordsof00ipsw%29.pdf).
* **Boston.** The old Common polygon at master pixel 5620,3420 lies in the water mask. The registered peninsula runs roughly from x5630–5660, y3398–3448. The 1630 town is a point. From 1645 the portion of the town occupying Shawmut is clipped to the old peninsula and closed across the Neck. The 1645 property reconstruction and Boston archaeology support the town core. Separate town holdings elsewhere are unresolved. [Boston property plans](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/71790) and [Boston Common archaeology](https://content.boston.gov/departments/archaeology/boston-common-archaeology).
* **Lancaster.** The English 1653 order ran five miles north, south and east and three west from a ford. The 1659 Noyes return describes a five-sided traverse. The west side was ten miles long, the north eight and the south six and a half. At Whipsuffrage the line changes direction. The published return gives both lengths adjoining the angled corner. The estimated ford is at the Nashua confluence on the artwork (4582,3210). Local scale is approximately 36 master pixels per mile, checked against the twin Waushacum ponds. Read Noyes’s compass directions as approximate sheet bearings. Magnetic variation, channel change and disagreement over the ford prevent parcel-level accuracy. The 1672 approval did not move the surveyed corners. [Lancaster records, pp. 65–68](https://brittlebooks.library.illinois.edu/brittlebooks_open/Books2009-08/lancas0001earrec/lancas0001earrec.pdf), [GNIS river junction](https://edits.nationalmap.gov/apps/gaz-domestic/public/summary/617991) and [Waushacum map](https://www.mass.gov/doc/east-waushacum-pond/download).
* **Connecticut River.** Some old municipal points are town centroids far from the settlement. On the artwork the old Northampton point was well west of the river corridor. Deerfield, Northampton and Springfield authority points have been relocated in relation to the Deerfield confluence, the Oxbow and the Westfield mouth respectively. They are locality symbols. The Schematics labeled Pocumtuc and Pocumtuc (Map B) cover the same people with incompatible reconstructions. Neither perimeter is carried through 1665. [UMass confluence photo and location](https://www.umass.edu/riversmart/gallery/deerfield-connecticut-river-confluence) and [Northampton town history](https://historicnorthampton.org/timeline/).
* **Northeast and far north.** The top-right of the master sheet contains insets over blank geography. The prior projected coordinates for Dover (6167,1264), Exeter (6053,1833), Hampton (6270,1877), Kittery (6492,1490) and York (6658,1364) all sample black in the master land mask. Placing a town or drawing the Saco or Penobscot shoreline there would mistake an inset for the scale of the main map. The political panel therefore carries the evidence by region. The northern Map B and Vermont overlays also acknowledge extrapolation. Descendant nation names and present-day core areas do not establish seventeenth-century polygons. [Champlain’s Saco plan](https://www.mainememory.net/record/33847), [NH state manual](https://sos.nh.gov/media/xisfh04s/a-history-of-new-hampshire-with-towns-cities.pdf) and [Penobscot Nation chronology](https://www.penobscotnation.org/services/cultural_historic_preservation/historic_preservation/tribal_timeline/index.php).
