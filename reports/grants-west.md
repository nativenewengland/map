# Hudson Valley, Manhattan and Long Island land-grant register

This addition contains 476 dated instruments. Hudson Valley and Manhattan have one polygon, 294 locality points and six inventory-only records. Long Island has six polygons, 166 locality points and three inventory-only records. These counts exclude records retained from the earlier authority layers. Several grants can occupy one locality or the same island. They are separate instruments with separate citations.

## Matching and map placement

Prior regional claims include Rombout/Rumbout, Rensselaerswyck/Rensselaerswijck, Livingston, Philipse/Philipsburg, Pelham/Pell, Cortlandt, New Paltz, Great Nine Partners, Beekman, Gardiners Island, East Hampton, Southampton, Smithtown and Southold. The main inventory audit identifies all 21 baseline rows and their aliases. A founding date, transfer of government or date of submission to Dutch rule does not identify a separate land patent. The 1666 Shelter Island instrument and the subsequent lordship have distinct register references. The island’s earlier ownership dispute remains visible in its original layer.

GG 58 and GG 88 are duplicate registrations with identical parties, date and measurements. HH 89 and HH 90 are successive instruments on the same day. The first was cancelled, and the second granted the marsh to New Utrecht. The day-resolution display includes the cancelled entry on its execution day with the cancellation explicit. GG 60 ended with the exchange for HH 135b. The southern Schuyler tract, Sanders–Harmense and Ten Eyck retain their overlapping claims. At Oyster Bay the 1653 deed expressly excepts Hog Island and Canteaiug. Contemporary Dutch area units are retained. Blank acreage means the cited evidence supplied no secure area.

Dutch dates use the Gregorian calendar. English dates generally advance ten days, with January through 24 March evaluated for the contemporary legal year. Uncertain month and year entries retain their recorded precision. Locality symbols may coincide at town scale. Whole-island instruments on Pagganck and Fishers follow the source artwork’s land mask. Shelter Island uses the earlier island outline. Tracts defined principally by adjoining owners, vanished trees and fences, unknown compass origins or obsolete place names retain points or inventory-only descriptions.

## Specific open series and records

- New York State Archives A4684/A4686 and 12943, 1664–1700, all in-scope counties. Check handwritten index leaves under variant spellings and county/place names and match each reference to the register image. Published excerpts cover only part of this series. This is the principal outstanding English source.
- Unregistered Dutch groundbriefs, especially September 1651–February 1654. Gehring’s introduction identifies eleven extant exceptions, including Albany and Long Island examples. The statewide register has a gap. Gehring’s HH 129 refers to nine original grants outside the translated sequence. Check their independent manuscript copies and Albany town, West India Company and private collections. Check defective or omitted HH items including 87, 97 and 98. [Guide to Dutch manuscripts](https://newnetherlandinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/A_Guide_to_Dutch_Manuscripts_Relating_to_New_Netherland_in_United_States_Repositories_2015_update.pdf)
- Albany/Rensselaerswyck, Beverwyck, Schenectady and Esopus/Wildwyck, 1630–1700. The proprietor’s manuscripts, local deed and court books and Schenectady proprietors’ minute books require item-level review for grants absent from GG/HH. The translated provincial register covers only recorded provincial instruments.
- Kings and Queens, 1645–1700. Work through all relevant NYC Old Town ledgers for Brooklyn, Bushwick, Flatbush, Flatlands, Gravesend, New Utrecht, Jamaica, Newtown and Flushing. The three preserved Gravesend original patents and the Dutch provincial register cover only part of their respective town series. Review the complete Hempstead and Oyster Bay town books for divisions after the transcribed sections. [NYC collection](https://www.archives.nyc/blog/2019/10/4/the-colonial-old-town-ledgers-digitization-project)
- Suffolk, 1640–1700. The entire proprietors’ books for Huntington, Brookhaven, Smithtown, Southold, Southampton, East Hampton and the South Fork, including local divisions, still require folio-by-folio comparison. Resolve the precise March date and legal year in the Queen’s Village/Lloyd Neck original and compare the conflicting proprietors’ names in accounts of the Horse Neck patent. Also verify the June day assigned to the 1697 St. George annexation. The electronic sources consulted disagree.
- Westchester and Orange/Ulster, 1640–1700. Compare the county deed registers and town/proprietor books for Yonkers, Rye, Mamaroneck, Eastchester, New Rochelle, Westchester, Kingston and New Paltz; resolve private releases, overlapping claims, successor tracts and original licenses to purchase against provincial executions.
- Read the original Fishers Island patent recitals for named Indigenous parties. The historical collections report a 1644 purchase and the original deed is missing. Verify Furman’s neighboring undated Oyster Bay survey entries against the town book and investigate the impossible printed year on the Manhattan Loockermans confirmation.

For day-boundary review select 15 and 16 June 1637 for Pagganck, 9 and 10 June 1666 for the first English Shelter Island instrument, 10 and 11 July 1670 for the Gravesend confirmation, and 25 and 26 May 1699 for revoked grants.
