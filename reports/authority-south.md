# Southern New England political geography, 1500–1700

The date control uses the Gregorian calendar. The exact English civil dates used by these southern records (1602–1692) require adding ten days and, for a January–March date entered under the March 25 year start, normalizing the year first. The selection after a year-only or month-only change represents the source period. The beginning of that period is a display convention. A settlement symbol identifies the locality of an attested authority. It does not delimit its land. The code contains the individual source links and evidence for each footprint.

## Regional checklist

| Area | Opening and contact | Establishment of colonial jurisdictions | Later changes and remaining uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Connecticut River | The sixteenth-century record supplies no town-by-town jurisdiction. Sequassen, Sowheage and later family signatories appear in local transactions. | The West India Company and Plymouth traders established separate posts in 1633. English Windsor, Wethersfield and Hartford entered common government in January 1639. Saybrook began as a separate fortified settlement and transferred to Connecticut in December 1644. | Underhill seized the Dutch House of Hope in 1653. Connecticut sequestered it in 1654. The Dominion displaced Connecticut’s charter government in 1687 and that government resumed in 1689. Wangunk and Nehantic transactions leave the ordering of particular tracts unresolved. |
| Housatonic, Quinnipiac and the western Sound | Sixteenth-century occupation evidence alone cannot separate the powers of all the river communities. The 1638 Quinnipiac agreement identifies Momauguin and his council. | New Haven began in April 1638. Its occupied nine-square plot is shown by 1641. Milford, Branford, Stratford and Fairfield are identified locally. The Quinnipiac agreement reserved land and imposed conditions on continued resource use. | The 1659 Golden Hill reserve and the overlapping Potatuck/Paugussett signatories in the 1670 Derby deed limit any reconstruction of exclusive jurisdiction. The New Haven committee submitted to Connecticut on January 15, 1665, before the joint May election. See the southwest Sound records for Guilford, Norwalk and neighboring communities. |
| Thames, Mystic and Pawcatuck | Pequot tribal history places the ancestral homeland between the Niantic and Pawcatuck rivers. The museum’s early-seventeenth-century village core lies between the Thames and Pawcatuck. The northern boundary and extent toward Weekapaug are uncertain. | Trading and tribute in the 1620s extended beyond the home villages. Uncas led a separate Mohegan polity at Shantok. The Pequot War, the destruction of Mistick and pursuit of refugees broke up the former core. The Hartford treaty imposed forced dispersal, tribute and restrictions on residence. | Cassacinamon’s followers established a community at Noank in 1651 and at Mashantucket in 1666. The Eastern Pequots’ 1683 deed identifies a distinct reserve at Lantern Hill. English Nameaug/New London and Norwich developed nearby. Recorded acreage is insufficient to draw any of the three Pequot reserve boundaries. |
| Narragansett Bay and south coast | Narragansett and Niantic histories establish long occupation. The people encountered in April 1524 could have been Narragansett or Wampanoag. The 1630s furnish evidence of sachems, tribute and protection. Ninigret exercised separate diplomatic authority into the 1670s. | Providence’s 1636 settlement and 1638 house lots, Portsmouth, Newport, Shawomet/Warwick and the four-town assembly mark local government. Aquidneck and Conanicut had a separate government in the early 1650s. Transfer of a deed and adoption of a patent or charter are distinct events. | Pettaquamscutt and Atherton purchases, competing charters, the King’s Province commission and Westerly’s incorporation all affected Narragansett country. Refugees, the Great Swamp Massacre, the burning of Providence and the postwar town meeting changed the extent and conditions of residence. Narragansett and Niantic communities survived. The exact inland limits of each jurisdiction remained unsettled in 1700. |
| Sowams and the eastern Bay | Wampanoag communities had their own sachems. The 1621 alliance concerned Ousamequin of Pokanoket. The Sowams footprint approximates the political center around the Warren and Bristol peninsulas. | The 1653 purchase excluded the Mount Hope lands. Wamsutta succeeded Ousamequin in 1661 and Metacom succeeded Wamsutta in 1662. | During the war, Pocasset under Weetamoo and Sakonnet under Awashonks followed different courses. In 1676 Metacom’s death produced competing colonial claims. Plymouth subsequently sold the Mount Hope lands in 1680. Separate proprietors’ divisions reached land formerly allotted to Awashonks in 1681. The extent of surviving Wampanoag landholding cannot be derived from the military outcome. |
| Patuxet, Buzzards Bay, Cape Cod | Patuxet appears as a community before the epidemic of 1616–19. At Nauset and Monomoy, Champlain’s observations, the Billington encounter, and the history of other lower Cape villages identify local communities with different degrees of autonomy. | Plymouth’s first building site gives way to the enclosed core in 1622. The 1652 Dartmouth purchase precedes recognition of Old Dartmouth in 1664. Plymouth families settled at Nauset in 1644, later Eastham. Mashpee leaders retained communal land by deed in 1665. | Wartime destruction interrupted Dartmouth settlement. The first surviving postwar meeting in 1678 documents municipal tax administration. Plymouth confirmed the Mashpee deed in 1685. The provincial government began operating in 1692. Cape-wide praying-town, village and town property bounds cannot be inferred from the documented centers. |
| Noepe, Nantucket and Elizabeth Islands | Tribal histories support Wampanoag communities on the islands. At island scale the shoreline locates those communities. Their internal political boundaries remain unresolved. The 1602 Cuttyhunk installation was occupied briefly. | The 1641 English proprietary instruments preceded actual English settlement. Separate Wampanoag authorities continued at Aquinnah and Chappaquiddick. Wanackmamack and Nickanoose dealt separately with Nantucket purchasers. | The Mayhew government under New York, the English town of Sherburne, continuing Wampanoag leadership and the 1692 change to Massachusetts appear separately. Wampanoag-language documents show Askammapoo acting through her son. A colonial grant never supplies an island-wide end date for Indigenous government. |

## Geographic registration

The land mask is an 8,192-pixel square. Local riverbanks and islands give better anchors for small political features than a single regional transformation. The previous Hartford location fell east of the Connecticut. The replacement places the English town and Dutch Point on the west bank, at separate sites. The previous Portsmouth point fell on Prudence, Newport on Conanicut and the Edgartown point on Chappaquiddick. The revised locations fall on northern Aquidneck, southwestern Aquidneck and the Noepe side of Edgartown Harbor respectively.

New Haven's nine squares form a roughly half-mile plot northwest of the head of the harbor. The 1641 reconstruction identifies named house lots and the marketplace. Its modern perimeter corresponds to York, Grove, State and George Streets. The artwork omits West Creek and other features filled since settlement, so the perimeter can be located only approximately. The quadrilateral is wholly on land. The town’s administrative dependencies and the Quinnipiac reservation require separate records. [New Haven Museum map and location key](https://www.newhavenmuseum.org/library/research-guides/brockett-map-of-1641/) and [National Register description](https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/84001135.pdf).

Providence's original town lots ran from the east shore of the Great Salt River to the ridge. The historic district study gives lengths of 1,600–3,000 feet. The registered strip lies east of the western channel and west of the Seekonk arm. Its footprint remains wholly on land. The source's acreage estimates differ within the report, so the shape represents the strip of lots at illustration scale. After March 1676 the same outline identifies the area affected by destruction. An August 1676 town meeting demonstrates renewed local administration. [College Hill National Historic Landmark documentation](https://preservation.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur406/files/pdfs_zips_downloads/national_pdfs/providence/prov_college-hill-hd-nhl.pdf) and [Providence’s record of the August meeting](https://www.providenceri.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Apology_EO.pdf).

The Pequot core uses the Thames and Pawcatuck channels, the Sound shore and a schematic inland limit toward southern Preston and Griswold. The broader combined Pequot/Mohegan outline in the inherited CSV cannot serve both as a village core and a tributary network. The southern portion of the revised outline extends into water before renderer clipping. The Noepe and Nantucket envelopes isolate the islands against the mask. All land pixels in the main Noepe, Chappaquiddick and Nantucket components inside the local test windows fall within their envelopes. The rest of the clipped Noepe envelope picks up small coastal fragments. The clipped Nantucket envelope contains no additional land component. Cuttyhunk’s post is a point on the shore beside Westend Pond because the map omits the small fort islet.

## Interpretation and sources

- The Mashantucket Pequot Tribal Nation describes the larger homeland from the Niantic to the Pawcatuck. The museum’s battlefield study gives the Thames as the western edge of a core village zone and notes sources extending the eastern limit toward Weekapaug. The map uses the common core during the early seventeenth century and discusses the other extents. [Tribal history](https://www.mptn-nsn.gov/history/) and [Battlefields of the Pequot War](https://pequotwar.org/about/).
- The Hartford treaty names the English, Narragansett and Mohegan parties and attempts to prohibit a continuing Pequot name and homeland. Pequot communities re-established distinct seats under their own leaders. Dates for particular reserves show these later conditions. [Treaty text](https://pequotwar.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/Grade-8-Treaty-of-Hartford-Guiding-Questions-Avery.pdf), [Eastern Pequot account](https://www.easternpequottribalnation.org/history) and [Mohegan account of Uncas](https://www.mohegan.nsn.us/about/our-tribal-history/historical-figures/sachem-uncas).
- The Narragansett Tribe describes wartime alliance, while the National Park Service documents a neutrality agreement and the demands to surrender refugees. Both describe different obligations in 1675. The selected account changes after the assault on the winter settlement. Ninigret's diplomacy followed its own course. [Narragansett early history](https://narragansettindiannation.org/history/early/), [Roger Williams National Memorial](https://www.nps.gov/rowi/learn/historyculture/kingphilip.htm) and [Fisher and Silverman’s study of Ninigret](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7591/j.ctt5hh17c).
- The 1638 Quinnipiac agreement documents restrictions as well as continued fishing, hunting and planting. Similar regional terms cannot be transferred to a neighboring town without local evidence. The Derby deed involved people associated with both the Potatuck and Paugussett. [Quinnipiac agreement](https://peabody.yale.edu/sites/default/files/documents/public-education/08%20indigenous%2003%20indigenous%20atlantic.pdf) and [Derby deed analysis and transcription](https://www.bia.gov/sites/default/files/dup/assets/as-ia/ofa/petition/079_schagh_CT/079_fd.pdf).
- Plymouth's account of its alliance with Ousamequin establishes obligations between the parties. Aspinet of Nauset, Weetamoo of Pocasset and Awashonks of Sakonnet exercised distinct powers. Wampanoag accounts at Aquinnah and Mashpee document the continued existence of local governments. [Pokanoket alliance](https://plimoth.org/yath/unit-2/plymouth-pokanoket-alliance), [Wampanoag country and sachemships](https://plimoth.org/yath/unit-1/map-of-wampanoag-country-in-the-1600s), [Aquinnah](https://wampanoagtribe-nsn.gov/wampanoag-history) and [Mashpee timeline](https://mashpeewampanoagtribe-nsn.gov/timeline).
- The Rhode Island historic survey associates the King's Province with the commission of 1664. Its actual order appointing magistrates is dated March 20, 1664 in the English March-based year, corresponding to March 30, 1665 Gregorian. Thus on March 9 the competing charter claims still appear. Similarly, the January New Haven submission and the joint May General Court identify different stages of a merger. [Royal commissioners’ order](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/Collections_of_the_Massachusetts_Historical_Society_%28IA_collectionsofmas00mass_8%29.pdf) and [New Haven transfer chronology with references to the colonial records](https://archontology.org/nations/us/new_haven/01_polity.php).
- Governor Phips arrived with the charter on May 14 Old Style. The published council minutes put the public reading and his oath on May 16. The political change is keyed to the second event, May 26 Gregorian. [Published council minutes](https://www.colonialsociety.org/node/350) and [Massachusetts Archives collection description](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/archives/collections/mass-archives-collection.htm).

## Independent browser checks

The local Chromium checks used the actual date field and mouse clicks on the map at zoom 5. Nineteen date-and-place combinations returned the expected evidence: both island homelands in 1500, Sowams at the 1621 alliance, Pequot village country and the post-Mistick displacement, the House of Hope on either side of its seizure, New Haven before and after submission and on March 9, 1665, Dartmouth that same day, the three Providence phases, the two Mount Hope phases, the Mashpee confirmation and Plymouth before and after the 1692 provincial inauguration. The first Mount Hope clicks exposed an intercepting Toowooset text label. After the renderer's pane order changed, clicks there passed going forward and backward through August 21–22, 1676. Fresh clicks on the Pequot, New Haven and Providence polygons also passed with the revised renderer.

The regional file contains 109 dated records, comprising 15 polygon phases, 64 site phases and 30 regional accounts. Each of the site centers falls on the dry side of the artwork mask at the renderer's threshold. A separate mask test found the entire New Haven plot and Providence house-lot strip on land. These checks assess the supplied reconstruction and do not substitute for period surveys of missing parcel lines.
