# Political geography of the main sheet, 1500–1700

The default political layer contains 537 dated records across nine regions. Of these, 65 describe an area, 330 locate a site, and 142 have regional evidence only. Counts include successive phases at the same place. Open the regional evidence reader to see the authority, date precision, evidence for the extent, and linked sources even where the artwork cannot support geometry. Unshaded ground has no established perimeter in this reconstruction. It can include places with substantial evidence of habitation, diplomacy or land use.

## Audit of the earlier map

The original feature CSV contains 1,732 records, including 615 markers, 1,016 labels and 101 polygons. Eighty-two polygons were historical territory interpretations and 19 were planting plots. All 82 territory rings are distinct after normalization of the starting vertex and winding. Several represent overlapping interpretations of the same geography. Examples include Pocumtuc and Pocumtuc (Map B), Koas and Cowasuck, and the combined Pequot–Mohegan sketch alongside separate polity sketches. The Long Island thirteen-tribe scheme, northern Map B extrapolations, modern descendant-community labels and a Connecticut historical reconstruction referring to circa 1625 each have a different evidentiary basis. Displaying all of these for two centuries gave the appearance of continuity. All 82 remain available for comparison in the optional historical reference layer. The earlier small dated colonial overlay also mixed forts, municipal cores and larger jurisdictions. The new popup identifies the legal or geographic basis in each case.

The drawing has local distortion and decorative insets. Registration used the Hudson and East River junction, the Connecticut and its tributaries, the Shawmut Neck, Narragansett Bay islands, Fort Pond, the two forks of Long Island and the islands off Cape Cod. Corrected positions include Manhattan, the Hartford posts, Portsmouth, Newport, Edgartown and Boston. Pagganck was already present in the original artwork. Its small shoreline was missing from the land, coast and ocean overlays. The correction changes 133 pixels in the 8,192-square territory mask. Much of Maine, parts of New Hampshire and the upper Hudson lack reliable locations on this sheet. The reader and regional guide symbols expose their dated records without inventing coordinate precision.

## Reproduce changes

The slider uses Gregorian dates. Intervals include the starting date and exclude the ending date. Month- and year-only evidence uses the first selectable day of that period, with precision identified in the record. English Old Style dates require accounting for the historical legal year as well as the ten-day difference in this period. A date that ends an evidence interval can mark the limit of a reconstruction.
