# Northern land-grant search, 1620–1700

## Geographic interpretation

The York, Wells, Hampton, Dover and Piscataqua localities correspond to the inset or blank area documented in [the map registration audit](authority-north.md). A colonial description can identify a tract there without providing a usable position on the main sheet. The claims below use inventory entries. Wells and York are reported under Maine throughout their changing political affiliations. A single New Hampshire instrument, including two noncontiguous tracts in New Hampshire and Maine, appears once.

## Identified record gaps

Screened the complete 1620–1700 section in the searchable scan of [Wadleigh, Notable Events in the History of Dover](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/68/Notable_events_in_the_history_of_Dover%2C_New_Hampshire%2C_from_the_first_settlement_in_1623_to_1865_%28IA_notableeventsinh00wadle%29.pdf) and read the town orders and lot lists on pp. 23–76. The 1642 Back River division names 23 recipients. The Cochecho Marsh division includes two additional assignments to Baker and Waldron beyond their six-acre dividends. Consulted the entire six-town 1630–1670 register in [Merchant and Millwright, pp. 146–147](https://assets.historicnewengland.org/main/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/25120422/merchant-and-millwright.pdf). Separate votes by Exeter, Dover and Kittery frequently concern the same mill site. The Dover transcript identifies Starbuck as Wiggin’s associate at Cochecho second falls and Bradstreet at Quamphegan. The municipal 1651 conveyance independently recites the August 1649 date for Bellemies Bank. The municipal historian and the regional register disagree on the dating of the Oyster River order. Building contracts, evidence that a mill existed, and later sales were screened out. The York 1700 vote for a meetinghouse pew is separate from the Joseph Bane land allotment on the same page.

The [Dover municipal manuscript catalog](https://www.dover.nh.gov/government/city-operations/finance/city-clerk-tax-collection/historic-dover-records/) has eleven sections for 1657–1753 and twenty-one for 1694–1757. The first section of the latter volume was retrieved and contains the beginning of a manuscript index. A folio-by-folio examination of both manuscript volumes remains outstanding. The Exeter–Dover municipal division and later boundary settlements in Wadleigh pp. 25, 37, 41 require reconciliation against Massachusetts General Court orders. Dover's territorial claim recites a 1635 Indigenous transaction on the Lamprey, without identifying all parties or supplying its instrument.

The Wells study (pp. 4–5) reports a missing town book for about 1645–1670. Later clerk and proprietor copies preserve only some original instruments. York Deeds volumes beyond the cited folios and the later recitals in Wells proprietor book I (1716–1763) still require folio-level review for references to seventeenth-century allotments. The manuscript date and preceding year style of Thomas Gorges’s Ogunquit selection need verification against York Deeds I(ii), 5. Joseph Cross’s tract is dated ambiguously in the exhibit, which prints 1668–9 for an April transaction. Exeter, Dover and Portsmouth early town/proprietor books, 1620–1700, are also outstanding, as are provincial probate and county deed series for individual in-scope holdings. Canadian sub-concessions, surviving Abenaki instruments and competing land claims in the upper Connecticut basin require further review. This search log records the documents examined and cannot support an exhaustive northern inventory.
