# Southern land grants, 1620–1700

The 1620 Plymouth sketch and the three 1623 allotment lists have been checked through their last entries. The broader southern search is incomplete. The points shared by Plymouth householders locate the documented plantation. Each row identifies a holding in a particular division. The temporal inventory keeps the event visible after its date without establishing continuous occupancy by the named recipient.

### Connecticut River valley deed series

Harry Andrew Wright, *Indian Deeds of Hampden County*, pp. 11–115, transcribes Hampshire and Hampden registry, Northampton, Northfield and Sunderland town books and Pocumtuck Valley Memorial Association originals. Read the sequence from the July 1636 Agawam deed through the August 1687 Northfield deed, including reverses, mortgages, assignments, confirmations and the June 1695 assignment. The next principal instrument dates to 1724. Coverage includes Springfield, Tantiusques, Westfield, Northampton, Hadley, Hatfield, Deerfield, Sunderland, Northfield, Brookfield, Suffield and Enfield. The Springfield founding deed matches the existing Agawam record. Several later deeds identify reserved cornfields, seasonal use and fishing rights, or earlier security interests. Town-book volumes, registry books and unprinted records warrant separate searches beyond Wright’s selection. [Library of Congress digitized edition](https://www.loc.gov/item/05036823/)

## Outstanding record work

- **Massachusetts Bay and Province.** General Court volumes I–V (1628–1686), the 1686–1689 Dominion and 1692–1700 provincial records need a sustained review of locatable grants. Town/proprietor books for the Charles, Concord and Merrimack valleys, Essex, the Nipmuc interior and the upper Connecticut valley hold local divisions and subsequent layouts. The Dedham town record is available beyond the founding pages examined here.
- **Plymouth.** Court orders I–VI, 1633–1691, remaining tracts in XII, town records at Duxbury, Scituate, Marshfield, Taunton and Sandwich, and the *Old Colony Deeds* manuscript books need instrument-by-instrument comparison through 1691. Massachusetts provincial and county series continue the search to 1700. Individual deeds in the deed registers include private resales, mortgages and estate transfers alongside original grants. [State Archives collection and digital volumes](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/archives/collections/plymouth-records.htm)
- **Connecticut and New Haven.** Compare RG 001:003 colonial land records, especially *Deeds, Patents, &c.* II, against the grants, farms and town confirmations in colonial *Public Records* I–IV (1636–1700) and New Haven jurisdiction records (1638–1665). The colony-wide 1685 authorization alone gives no issue date for individual settlements. Lebanon Book I contains a further group of allotments of November 1695. Local volumes must resolve Fitch’s Mile and the resurveyed Lyme, Hartford, Branford, New London and other town tracts. [Connecticut archival indexes](https://libguides.ctstatelibrary.org/hg/ctarchives) · [Town holdings guide](https://libguides.ctstatelibrary.org/c.php?g=1495953&p=11173642)
- **Rhode Island.** Bartlett, *Records of the Colony* I–III and IV through 1700, early Providence records, Portsmouth and Newport proprietors’ books, Westerly and Warwick land evidence, Narragansett purchases, and the New Shoreham and Conanicut records require further review. In particular, check Westcott’s June 1659 Warwick deed and the Warwick register for 1668–1681 against later recordings.
- **The islands.** The 1620–1700 inspection of Dukes County, Edgartown, the Vineyard proprietary books, the Elizabeth Islands and New York’s island patents is incomplete. Nantucket index coverage is strongest for 1659–1684. Local and Albany instruments, especially 1685–1700, remain. MVD 1:386 dates a Wampanoag-language writing by registration to 1687; the grantor, extent and date of execution require interpretation.


### Narragansett land evidence and competing claims

The existing 1658–1665 Pettaquamscutt/Atherton article describes several companies and competing jurisdictions. The dated instrument rows distinguish the Pettaquamscutt hill purchase and later enlargement from the Atherton deeds for Quidnessett and Boston Neck. Compared Potter pp. 55–61, Rhode Island Land Evidence I:164–165, 190 and II:147–155, 189–192 through Potter and Cole, including the Narragansett protest of September 1662. ### Connecticut township patent returns

Compared the 1685 General Court order and blank form (Public Records III, pp. 177–178) with the local instruments for Norwich, Windsor, Guilford, Milford, Fairfield, Stratford, Stamford, Norwalk, Branford and Middletown. Stratford names five islands in its postscript. Its May 1685 application, May 1686 seal and December 1688 local recording are distinct dates. Norwalk likewise applied in May 1685 and received its seal in July 1686. The Middletown endorsement explicitly gives the civil year 1685/86. Milford obtained a replacement patent in 1713 after expanding its bounds. The cited New Haven and Stonington patents date to 1704 and 1716 respectively. The existing authority entries for the founding of these towns describe earlier events. [Colonial order](https://www.cga.ct.gov/hco/books/The_Public_Records_of_the_Colony_of_Connecticut_1678-1689.pdf) · [Lambert’s transcriptions](https://www.cga.ct.gov/hco/books/History_of_the_Colony_of_New_Haven.pdf) · [Stratford record](https://ldsgenealogy.com/CT/books/Reports-and-papers-Fairfield-County-Historical-Society-Bridgeport-Conn-1882-1896-97-part-24.htm) · [Middletown record](https://cga.ct.gov/hco/books/History_of_Middlesex_County.pdf) · [Stonington patent](https://www.cga.ct.gov/hco/books/History_of_the_Town_of_Stonington_etc.pdf)
The issued Hartford patent of May 26 names actual proprietors including Talcott and Wyllys. The general court form omits their names. The Wethersfield confirmation of 1685/86 follows the separate 1672 colonial enlargement and 1673 deed. The Waterbury patent of February 1686/87 follows the naming in 1686. ### Massachusetts Bay General Court volume I

Downloaded the full searchable Shurtleff volume and searched the colonial business chronologically, the printed pp. 60–346, the index and the marginal headings for place names, grants, acreages, islands, meadow, mill, weir, plantation, town, farm, neck and layout terminology. Examined instruments around pp. 94–104, 114–120, 126–130, 139–149, 157, 165–180, 183, 191, 206–207, 222–229, 237–240, 262–263, 272–282, 289–295, 303–309, 324–331 and 343–346. These include the successive Boston harbor island grants, Salem and Mystic River farms, the Two Brothers grants, the early Pequot-country land claims, Nantasket fishing tracts, and later assignments around Sudbury and the Merrimack. Concord, Dedham and Watertown orders, the Saltonstall meadow and the Fishers Island award have existing instrument matches. Bare unsurveyed acreage awards on pp. 206, 222, 237, 240, 262, 307, 331 and 344 need reconciliation with later layouts before they can be assigned to a tract. Text recognition is defective in parts of the dated margins and in some quantities. This extraction does not exhaust the individual awards or layouts in the volume.
