# Hudson Valley, Manhattan and Long Island, 1500–1700

## Regional evidence checklist

| Region and period | Political change and geographic evidence | Remaining uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Upper Hudson and western edge, 1500–1620s | Stockbridge-Munsee history places the Mohican central fire at Schodack and communities along the tidal Hudson before Hudson’s 1609 voyage. Mohawk villages occupied the Mohawk Valley. Families also shared upland hunting grounds. Fort Nassau and then Fort Orange were separate trading footholds. [Mohican chronology](https://www.mohican.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Tribal-History-Timeline_Mohican.Com_.pdf), [Mohawk and Mohican upland use](https://home.nps.gov/articles/adirondacks-native-americans.htm), [Fort Orange archaeology](https://nysm.nysed.gov/exhibitions/ongoing/fort-orange) | No traced precontact division across the Adirondacks or surveyed upstream islands on this sheet. |
| Upper Hudson, 1620s–1664 | Conflict over the trade altered Mohican and Mohawk relations. Rensselaerswijck’s tenants and court surrounded the company post. In April 1652 the company asserted jurisdiction around Fort Orange and established a local court at Beverwijck. Mohawk grantors at Schenectady dealt separately with Van Curler before settlement in 1662. [Fort Orange records, introduction](https://www.newnetherlandinstitute.org/application/files/4616/8372/5016/Fort_Orange_Records_16561678.pdf), [Schenectady deed](https://schenectadyhistory.org/resources/mvgw/history/021.html) | The legal distance around the fort is known. Its projected position and local ground scale cannot locate that boundary reliably. |
| Upper Hudson and western edge, 1664–1700 | English and restored Dutch courts served Albany, Rensselaerswijck and Schenectady. Mohican-led Highlands alliances, the Schaghticoke refugee community and the Albany Covenant Chain councils document Native political reorganization. Dongan chartered Albany in 1686. Its magistrates opposed Leisler in 1689–90. A French and Indigenous raid destroyed much of Schenectady in 1690. [Court records](https://www.newnetherlandinstitute.org/application/files/4616/8372/5016/Fort_Orange_Records_16561678.pdf), [Hoosic valley history](https://www.williamstownhistoricalmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/Hoosic_History.pdf), [New York State Museum](https://nysm.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/onondaga_and_empire_a.pdf) | Schaghticoke accounts date Andros’s symbolic tree to either 1675 or 1676. Its location at the Hoosic–Tomhannock confluence is clear in real geography. The base map omits those tributaries. |
| Middle Hudson, 1500–1658 | Broad Lenape and Mohican histories provide the early frame. Seventeenth-century evidence locates Esopus communities west and Wappinger communities east of the river. Dutch farms on the Esopus floodplain preceded the uphill stockade of 1658. [Cultural resource survey](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=%7BC8F24162-F1BD-4EF4-B054-92AC3D00D0D1%7D), [Persen House excavations](https://clerk.ulstercountyny.gov/sites/default/files/resources/Persen%20House%20Archaeological%20Excavations%20Vol.1%2C%202004_compressed.pdf) | Later names and ranges cannot identify the local political line in 1500. The printed rivers give insufficient registration for the Kingston palisade. |
| Middle Hudson, 1659–1700 | The two Esopus wars, 1664 Dutch peace, and October 1665 Nicolls agreement have distinct entries. Renewals evidence continued Esopus diplomatic authority. The 1677 New Paltz patent and Wappinger agreement of 1683/Rombout patent of 1685 have separate legal histories. The 1689 Rombout survey still shows Native fields. [Ulster County treaty](https://clerk.ulstercountyny.gov/sites/default/files/resources/Nicolls%20Esopus%20Peace%20Treaty_2015.pdf), [Dutchess County historical survey](https://www.dutchessny.gov/Graphics/Transformations_of_an_American_County_1683-1983_ocr.pdf) | Fishkill and Wappingers Creek bound the Rombout deed in part. Their mouths are missing from the western artwork. |
| Manhattan and lower estuary, 1500–1653 | Manahatta and Pagganck were part of a wider Lenape homeland. The 1624 company settlement on Nutten Island moved to Manhattan. Schaghen’s 1626 report supplies neither signers nor terms for the claimed purchase. Northern Manhattan Native communities continued. Kieft’s War disrupted outlying Dutch farms and Lenape settlements. [National Museum of the American Indian](https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/manhattan/native-people/native-people.cshtml), [Schaghen letter](https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/manhattan/schaghen-letter/schaghen-letter.cshtml), [Governors Island history](https://www.nps.gov/gois/learn/historyculture/a-brief-history-of-governors-island.htm) | The island shoreline locates a part of Lenape country. It cannot supply internal sachemship boundaries or the terms of Indigenous participation in the 1626 agreement. |
| Lower estuary, 1653–1700 | The Wall Street palisade bounds the lower urban core. Nieuw Haarlem, Bergen and Oostdorp had separate local histories. In 1665 Nicolls incorporated the island’s inhabitants in a municipal government. In 1686 Dongan expressly defined Manhattan’s jurisdiction by the island’s low-water limits. During the 1673 restoration the New Orange fort and later Haarlem officials appear separately. Leisler’s control of the fort in 1689–91 coexisted with chartered city government. [Palisade account](https://home.nps.gov/afbg/learn/historyculture/african-burial-ground-in-history.htm), [Municipal charter texts](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a3/Treatise_upon_the_estate_and_rights_of_the_corporation_of_the_city_of_New_York%2C_as_proprietors_%28IA_cu31924070686773%29.pdf), [Dutch local oaths](https://encyclopedia.nahc-mapping.org/document/minute-qualifying-magistrates-several-towns) | The historic low-water line has shifted. The artwork supplies the geographic approximation. The pre-wall Dutch fort remains a point. Staten Island's early failed settlements, Oude Dorp, the 1670 collective deed and Richmond County in 1683 have separate records. The western artwork truncates the island. |
| Western and central Long Island, 1500–1664 | Shinnecock oral history describes linked villages with local sachems. The nineteenth-century thirteen-tribe scheme gives a misleading appearance of surveyed districts. Western sachems made peace after Kieft’s War, and Tackapousha represented an alliance in 1656. Breuckelen’s 1646 court, English-speaking towns chartered by New Netherland, the 1650 Hartford division and the 1663–64 revolts have distinct records. [Shinnecock Nation](https://www.shinnecock-nsn.gov/who-we-are), [Western alliance and the proposed fort](https://nysarchaeology.org/download/nysaa/bulletin/number_108.pdf), [Breuckelen council ordinance](https://encyclopedia.nahc-mapping.org/document/dutch-colonial-council-minutes-12-june-1646), [Local courts](https://history.nycourts.gov/about_period/local-courts-in-new-netherland/) | The protection agreement names participants. It provides no defensible outer perimeter or proof of the proposed Dutch fort’s construction. |
| Western and central Long Island, 1664–1700 | New York’s town courts, the 1673 summons, separate local oaths, and the return of English administration replace each other. The 1683 act names the towns in Kings, Queens and Suffolk. Nissequogue and Huntington claims overlapped at Smithtown. Andros confirmed a township in 1677. The 1683 Connetquot purchase and 1700 Unkechaug reserved planting lands are recorded separately. [Duke’s Laws](https://history.nycourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Publications_Dukes-Transcript-compressed.pdf), [County act](https://empirestateplaza.ny.gov/1683-act), [Unkechaug land records and landscape](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-I29-PURL-gpo186500/pdf/GOVPUB-I29-PURL-gpo186500.pdf) | The Islip precinct dates to 1710. Current precinct or reservation boundaries cannot identify seventeenth-century governing extent. |
| North Fork, 1500–1700 | Corchaug archaeological evidence establishes a fortified seventeenth-century site in the Cutchogue vicinity. At Hashamomuck, private farms and separate deeds preceded a dispute over New Haven taxation. Southold operated first as a branch of New Haven and then a partner. The 1661 allotments and 1676 Andros patent preceded later county and town divisions. The patent named Wading Creek, the Sound, the Peconic waters and Plum Island. [Fort Corchaug](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/74001308), [Southold town historian](https://www.southoldtownny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/12030/European-Settlement-of-Southold), [Town patent](https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2019/04/southoldamend.pdf) | The palisade’s site is protected. A locality marker preserves its geographic context. The North Fork plot approximates the western line, where inland headwaters and survey marks are indistinct. |
| South Fork, 1500–1664 | Shinnecock stewardship, tribute to the Pequot recorded in 1628, the 1640 Southampton agreement, and succession among local sachems appear separately. East Hampton’s 1648 deed reserved resource rights across the land between Southampton and Napeague. Later grazing agreements and land transactions reached Fort Pond. The Shinnecock Nation recounts Quashawam’s complaint over unpaid occupation. [Shinnecock Nation](https://www.shinnecock-nsn.gov/who-we-are), [BIA historical evidence](https://www.bia.gov/sites/default/files/media_document/004_pf.pdf), [Town records](https://ehtrustees.com/about/history/1600s/), [Montaukett land-use study, figure 4.2](https://preservationlongisland.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/McGovern-2015-Disrupting-the-Narrative_-Labor-and-Survivance-for-the-Montaukett.pdf) | East Hampton’s western limit in 1648 refers to Southampton’s possessions. The drawing uses their later staked division as an approximation. Hither Woods and the nine-score-acre purchase lack secure corners at this scale. |
| South Fork and islands, 1664–1700 | East Hampton’s Nicolls and Dongan patents and Southampton’s Andros and Dongan patents establish town authority. Montaukett families retained residence east of Fort Pond. In 1673 eastern delegates negotiated with the Dutch, but Southampton’s town meeting refused its oath. Gardiners Island passed from estate to autonomous jurisdiction and, in 1686, a manor with its own courts. Shelter Island is documented separately in the island records. [East Hampton patents](https://dos.ny.gov/town-east-hampton-lwrp-document), [Southampton documents](https://www.southamptontownny.gov/207/Historic-Documents), [Southampton’s refusal](https://encyclopedia.nahc-mapping.org/document/answer-town-southampton-refusing-swear-allegiance-dutch-government), [Gardiner manorial grant](https://www.newyorkfamilyhistory.org/knowledgebase/manors-new-york-part-one) | Evidence for Dutch superior authority over Gardiners Island during the restoration remains unresolved. The 1703 Shinnecock lease and later Montauk allocations lie beyond this timeline. |

## Registration and reproducible changes

On the zoom-5 artwork the legacy Manhattan core around **(250, 6929)** lies inland west of the Hudson. The Battery is near **(431, 7199)**, with the East River on its eastern side. The replacement island polygon follows the connected Manhattan land pixels in the mask, simplified to about two artwork pixels. Town centers on western Long Island were relocated relative to the East River, Jamaica Bay and the registered Long Island place-name layer. The old Brooklyn marker was also on the New Jersey side. The political layer uses the corrected positions.

On the South Fork, Fort Pond is immediately west of the larger Lake Montauk. The pasture and residence polygons use that pond vicinity and the two shores. The East Hampton polygon follows the Sag Harbor–Wainscott division westward and excludes Gardiners Island. Southampton uses the Seatuck and Peconic landmarks. Southold uses the historic Wading Creek name Pahquahkossit and the North Fork shore through Plum Island. Land geometry on the illustrated sheet is generalized. The audit found that the original land mask omitted Pagganck despite the island being visible in the colored artwork. The corrected mask and hydrography restore its zoom-6 shore, and the 1624 settlement remains a point. A mask-intersection check found zero shared land pixels between the East Hampton or Southold jurisdiction and Shelter Island or Gardiners Island. Southampton and East Hampton share their specified Sag Harbor–Wainscott edge.

Each map record supplies its own source and evidence. In intervals labelled unresolved the missing item may be the calendar of a local surrender, a survey corner, the scope of a named sachem’s authority, or the date at which title became effective local government. Those gaps affect different parts of the reconstruction.
