## Coverage questions

- **Ottawa, St. Lawrence and Wabanaki country** — Stadacona and Hochelaga are recorded in the 1530s. Champlain's 1603 journey does not establish them as continuing seats. Political affiliations in the northern interior remain unresolved. [Source](https://ontarioarchaeology.org/wp-content/uploads/OA_web_100.pdf)
- **Great Lakes and northern New York** — The Deer nation's admission to the Wendat confederacy is usually placed around 1610. The reconstruction distinguishes that later membership. Some western seats are beyond the terrain frame. [Source](https://www.asau.ru/files/pdf/2036197.pdf)
- **Southern New England and Long Island** — The Hassanamisco history also identifies Pegan and Nipnet. Their separate seats and jurisdiction around 1600 require further evidence. [Source](https://nipmucnation.org/history)
- **Southern New England and Long Island** — Nantucket accounts identify Wanackmamack, Nicornoose, Autopscot and Potconet in connection with 1659. The distribution of authority among their predecessors in 1600 remains open. [Source](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/Nantucket%3B_a_history_%28IA_nantuckethistory01doug%29.pdf)
- **Hudson, Delaware and Susquehanna valleys** — Kechemeckes, Little Siconese, Asomoches, Armewamese, Axion, Mosilian, Sewapois, Narraticons, Okanickon and Minguannan also occur in later records. Their circa-1600 seats and relationships have yet to be established here. [Source](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Places/America/United_States/_Topics/history/_Texts/WESDDV/5*.html)
- **Hudson, Delaware and Susquehanna valleys** — The archaeological labels Shenks Ferry and Monongahela do not establish a single polity for each settlement area. The western and central Pennsylvania interior remains an evidence gap. [Source](https://npshistory.com/publications/choh/aie-v1-2011.pdf)
- **Chesapeake, Delmarva and Virginia Piedmont** — The Monacan Nation records Tauxnitanias, Shackaconias, Outponcas, Tegoneaes, Whonkentyaes, Stegarakes and Hassinnungas in the 1608 account of Mannahoac tribute. Individual seats await defensible placement. [Source](https://www.monacannation.gov/step-back-in-time.html)
- **Chesapeake, Delmarva and Virginia Piedmont** — The 1607 reconstruction identifies 28–32 groups under Wahunsenacawh. Membership changed through conquest and diplomacy. The map distinguishes the six inherited districts from later evidence. [Source](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/powhatan-d-1618/)
- **Chesapeake, Delmarva and Virginia Piedmont** — Opiscopank, lower and upper Cuttatawomen, Pissaseck, Nantaughtacund and Onawmanient are individually named in the 1608 voyage region. Local placements and political affiliations at the target date remain under review. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/getinvolved/upload/SignificanceStatement-2.pdf)
- **Delmarva · researched, extent unresolved** — Tockwogh and Ozinies/Wicomiss: the NPS distinguishes plausible town sites, a river association, and contested land. Which areas between the Chester and Sassafras were governed by each polity in 1600? [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/CAJO_FINAL-ICL-REPORT_2023_0314-508.pdf)
- **Southern Delmarva · researched, authority unresolved** — Accomack and Accohannock have separately documented cores. The strength of their political relationship at the target date and the northern reach toward Pocomoke remain open. [Source](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/pdf_files/AC-081_Historic_AH_Survey_Eastern_Shore_2017_CRAI_report.pdf)
- **St. Lawrence · researched, seasonal use unresolved** — Iroquet met allies near the Richelieu but wintered upriver. The meeting place cannot supply the location or extent of a homeland. [Source](https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/iroquet_1F.html)
- **Western Long Island and lower Hudson · researched, extent unresolved** — Canarsee, Tappan and Wiechquaeskeck appear in later records. Which contact-period land-use areas correspond to political jurisdiction before colonial land transactions? [Source](https://www.nan.usace.army.mil/Portals/37/Appendix%20A8_Tier%201%20Cultural%20Resources_SEP2022.pdf)
- **Middle Eastern Shore · researched, earlier affiliation unresolved** — A 1669 act locates Choptank land on the south bank. It postdates the target by nearly seventy years. Distinct Choptank government and its relationship to Kuskarawock in 1600 cannot be reconstructed from that tract. [Source](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccolm/m3000/m3100/m3173/pdf/m3173-1777.pdf)
- **Georgian Bay · researched, internal division unresolved** — Bear and Cord were founding Wendat nations. The Penetanguishene assessment places its study site in Bear territory, but assigning the intervening shores to Bear, Cord or Rock around 1600 requires contemporary settlement sequences. The sketch includes the Trent outlet, Rice Lake and the Kawartha lakes. Its inland margin is approximate. Oral tradition records periods of withdrawal and return. This shoreline homeland overlaps other nations’ uses, and its political divisions around 1600 remain unresolved. [Source](https://lennox-addington.on.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/21EA-099-Stage-1-AA_RE_2022-Mar-18.pdf) |
| Wepawaug | Political outline | The Wepawaug occupied the coastal district around Milford east of the Housatonic mouth and upstream toward lower Derby, west of the Quinnipiac. The archaeological synthesis distinguishes them from Paugussett at Great Neck. It describes colonial-era claims farther inland whose antiquity is uncertain. Only the estuary and coastal core is shown. [Source](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/DECD/Historic-Preservation/03_Technical_Assistance_Research/Research/HPCT-IV_WesternUplands.pdf) |
| Mashteuiatsh, Essipit and Pessamit (shared Nitassinan) | Ancestral homeland | The three Innu First Nations identify a shared ancestral homeland along the north bank of the St. Lawrence from the Saint-Maurice to the Saguenay. Upland tributaries, including those in the Laurentides reserve and Grands-Jardins, support the inland reconstruction. The joint agreement describes ancestral relationships retrospectively. It does not resolve political divisions among headmen in 1600. [Source](https://portsaguenay.ca/donnees/media/fichiers/202406_PortSag_GuideReference_Anglo_PRINT.pdf) |
| Ardoch Algonquin First Nation (family wintering country) | Ancestral homeland | Ardoch relates its origins to Algonquin families wintering in the uplands where the Madawaska, Mississippi and Rideau watersheds approach one another. This generalization covers the interior lake district around Ardoch and the upper Mississippi. The nation names longstanding use, without assigning a circa-1600 political perimeter or identifying those families with Iroquet. [Source](https://www.aafna.ca/) |
| Kanienkehaka (Mohawk) northern hunting country | Ancestral homeland | The cultural resources study cites Fenton and Tooker and Day for Mohawk hunting in portions of the southern Adirondacks and lower Lake Champlain valley. Western Abenaki occupation is recorded on the eastern shore. The approximate polygon follows the southern Adirondack foothills north toward the southern Champlain reach. It represents hunting use with contested margins. Principal Mohawk settlements lay farther south along the Mohawk River. [Source](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=%7BD7D0DAD5-082C-4475-86F6-B211DB2E276B%7D) |
| Choptank | Political outline | The Maryland synthesis identifies a distinct contact-period polity occupying several towns on the southern Choptank shore between Cambridge and Secretary Creek. Inland, its lands included the headwaters of the Little Blackwater and Transquaking. This reconstruction follows those limits. The later treaty of 1659 confirms a political identity but does not establish the precise 1600 border. [Source](https://roads.maryland.gov/OED/Native_American_Trails.pdf) |
| Assateague | Political outline | Maryland identifies the Assateague as an Atlantic Delmarva people. Historic travel accounts and settlement evidence place the coastal community around Brockatonorton Bay and the upper Marshall Creek area near Newark. The outline follows that occupied mainland coastal reach. The absence of early colonial records leaves its inland extent and possible subdivisions uncertain. The later Snow Hill reservation is excluded. [Source](https://roads.maryland.gov/OED/Native_American_Trails.pdf) |
| Rockawalkin | Locality or reference | Tundotank near modern Salisbury anchors this record on the Wicomico River. The Maryland synthesis and Pocomoke Indian Nation distinguish these people from the Wicomiss of the Chester. Mid-seventeenth-century accounts establish the locality, but their political relationship with the Nanticoke and their territorial reach in 1600 are uncertain. [Source](https://roads.maryland.gov/OED/Native_American_Trails.pdf) |
| Annemessex | Locality or reference | Documented in the Crisfield area and later associated with the Pocomoke paramountcy. Its prominence and lands around 1600 cannot be reconstructed from the later treaty and reservation descriptions. [Source](https://roads.maryland.gov/OED/Native_American_Trails.pdf) |
| Abenaki homelands (Ndakinna) | Ancestral homeland | The pre-1600 synthesis places Abenaki communities across the Lake Champlain, Connecticut, Merrimack and Androscoggin districts. This generalized inland core includes the upper Connecticut and the White Mountains and extends into the southern Quebec uplands. The rim approximates the overlap among community homelands. Separate Missisquoi, Cowasuck, Pennacook and other political cores represent local affiliations. [Source](https://moose.nhhistory.org/units/abenaki/abenaki) |
| W8banaki (northern Ndakina) | Ancestral homeland | Northern portion of Ndakina encompassing the Saint-François and Chaudière country. National accounts identify the St. Lawrence, Richelieu–Champlain and Etchemin river limits. The west was shared with Kanien’kehá:ka people. The sketch ends across the northern New England uplands for this regional view; W8banaki lands extend south of it. This ancestral geography leaves local sachemship limits around 1600 unresolved. [Source](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/action-plans/richelieu-river-multi-species-final-2025.html) |
| Kitcisakik and Lac Simon Anicinapek (shared use) | Ancestral homeland | The assessment records combined traditional use by Kitcisakik and Lac Simon, including the Akasaba area. This sketch links Grand Lac Victoria and the upper Ottawa lake country to Lac Simon and the Akasaba area. Most of the shared territory continues north of this sheet. The source describes ancestral and more recent use. It supplies no political division between these communities in 1600. [Source](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80090/121712E.pdf) |
| Lower Cuttatawomen | Political outline | Smith distinguished this lower Rappahannock polity from the group of the same name farther upstream in 1608. The NPS town inventory locates its settlements at Mosquito Point, west of the modern Route 3 crossing, Orchard Point and near Bertrand. This is an approximate near-contact bank reach; its interior extent is unknown. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/ICL-Rappahannock-508.pdf) |
| Pissaseck | Political outline | The NPS synthesis assigns several early towns to Pissaseck, including the seat at Leedstown, Mangoraca near Smith Mount Landing and Wecuppom near Fones Cliffs. The outline covers that series of occupied northeast-bank reaches. Using Smith’s 1608 settlement geography leaves the inland frontier and exact affiliations in 1600 uncertain. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/ICL-Rappahannock-508.pdf) |
| Nandtaughtacund | Political outline | In 1608 Smith distinguished this polity from its upstream Cuttatawomen neighbors. Its chief’s town lay at Portobago Bay and associated places extended to Skinker’s Neck, Goldenvale Creek and across the river near Cleve. The shape approximates the inhabited reaches on both banks. No outer inland boundary or political dependence circa 1600 is established. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/ICL-Rappahannock-508.pdf) |
| Upper Cuttatawomen | Political outline | A second Cuttatawomen polity occupied the tidal Rappahannock upstream of the Nandtaughtacund. The NPS inventory assigns it towns at Hop Yard Landing, Moss Neck and Popcastle Creek on both sides of the river. Their positions on Smith’s near-contact map support the riverfront core. The inland limits are estimated. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/ICL-Rappahannock-508.pdf) |
| Piankatank | Political outline | Smith distinguished the people of the Piankatank in his early account. The Greater York study identifies their country adjoining this tidal river between the Chesapeake and Dragon Swamp. The outline approximates the inhabited tidal corridor. Later Kiskiack refuge lands and the wider swamp do not fix its earlier interior boundary. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/ICL-YorkPamunkeyMattaponi-508.pdf) |
| Potapoco (Potapaco) | Political outline | This local polity maintained three settlements on the Port Tobacco River. Smith sailed upstream to the community in 1608, before English settlement of Maryland. The outline follows the tidal river valley at and downstream of the early seat, with approximate inland sides. The later migration to Virginia is outside this reconstruction. [Source](https://2017mdmanual.msa.maryland.gov/msa/mdmanual/01glance/native/html/01native.html) |
| Nanjemoy | Political outline | This Piscataway-affiliated community occupied the Maryland bank of the Potomac between Mallows Bay and Nanjemoy Creek. Smith’s mapped Nushemouck anchors the identification near contact. The shoreline and creek fix the riverward core. The more inland side is approximate. [Source](https://2017mdmanual.msa.maryland.gov/msa/mdmanual/01glance/native/html/01native.html) |

### Regional questions

- **Ottawa, St. Lawrence and Wabanaki country**. Stadacona and Hochelaga are recorded in the 1530s. Champlain's 1603 journey does not establish them as continuing seats. Political affiliations in the northern interior remain unresolved. [Source](https://ontarioarchaeology.org/wp-content/uploads/OA_web_100.pdf)
- **Great Lakes and northern New York**. The Deer nation's admission to the Wendat confederacy is usually placed around 1610. The reconstruction distinguishes that later membership. Some western seats are beyond the terrain frame. [Source](https://www.asau.ru/files/pdf/2036197.pdf)
- **Southern New England and Long Island**. The Hassanamisco history also identifies Pegan and Nipnet. Their separate seats and jurisdiction around 1600 require further evidence. [Source](https://nipmucnation.org/history)
- **Southern New England and Long Island**. Nantucket accounts identify Wanackmamack, Nicornoose, Autopscot and Potconet in connection with 1659. The distribution of authority among their predecessors in 1600 remains open. [Source](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/Nantucket%3B_a_history_%28IA_nantuckethistory01doug%29.pdf)
- **Hudson, Delaware and Susquehanna valleys**. Kechemeckes, Little Siconese, Asomoches, Armewamese, Axion, Mosilian, Sewapois, Narraticons, Okanickon and Minguannan also occur in later records. Their circa-1600 seats and relationships have yet to be established here. [Source](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Places/America/United_States/_Topics/history/_Texts/WESDDV/5*.html)
- **Hudson, Delaware and Susquehanna valleys**. The archaeological labels Shenks Ferry and Monongahela do not establish a single polity for each settlement area. The western and central Pennsylvania interior remains an evidence gap. [Source](https://npshistory.com/publications/choh/aie-v1-2011.pdf)
- **Chesapeake, Delmarva and Virginia Piedmont**. The Monacan Nation records Tauxnitanias, Shackaconias, Outponcas, Tegoneaes, Whonkentyaes, Stegarakes and Hassinnungas in the 1608 account of Mannahoac tribute. Individual seats await defensible placement. [Source](https://www.monacannation.gov/step-back-in-time.html)
- **Chesapeake, Delmarva and Virginia Piedmont**. The 1607 reconstruction identifies 28–32 groups under Wahunsenacawh. Membership changed through conquest and diplomacy. The map distinguishes the six inherited districts from later evidence. [Source](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/powhatan-d-1618/)
- **Chesapeake, Delmarva and Virginia Piedmont**. Opiscopank, lower and upper Cuttatawomen, Pissaseck, Nantaughtacund and Onawmanient are individually named in the 1608 voyage region. Local placements and political affiliations at the target date remain under review. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/getinvolved/upload/SignificanceStatement-2.pdf)
- **Delmarva · researched, extent unresolved**. Tockwogh and Ozinies/Wicomiss: the NPS distinguishes plausible town sites, a river association, and contested land. Which areas between the Chester and Sassafras were governed by each polity in 1600? [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/CAJO_FINAL-ICL-REPORT_2023_0314-508.pdf)
- **Southern Delmarva · researched, authority unresolved**. Accomack and Accohannock have separately documented cores. The strength of their political relationship at the target date and the northern reach toward Pocomoke remain open. [Source](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/pdf_files/AC-081_Historic_AH_Survey_Eastern_Shore_2017_CRAI_report.pdf)
- **St. Lawrence · researched, seasonal use unresolved**. Iroquet met allies near the Richelieu but wintered upriver. The meeting place cannot supply the location or extent of a homeland. [Source](https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/iroquet_1F.html)
- **Western Long Island and lower Hudson · researched, extent unresolved**. Canarsee, Tappan and Wiechquaeskeck appear in later records. Which contact-period land-use areas correspond to political jurisdiction before colonial land transactions? [Source](https://www.nan.usace.army.mil/Portals/37/Appendix%20A8_Tier%201%20Cultural%20Resources_SEP2022.pdf)
- **Middle Eastern Shore · researched, earlier affiliation unresolved**. A 1669 act locates Choptank land on the south bank. It postdates the target by nearly seventy years. Distinct Choptank government and its relationship to Kuskarawock in 1600 cannot be reconstructed from that tract. [Source](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccolm/m3000/m3100/m3173/pdf/m3173-1777.pdf)
- **Georgian Bay · researched, internal division unresolved**. Bear and Cord were founding Wendat nations. The Penetanguishene assessment places its study site in Bear territory, but assigning the intervening shores to Bear, Cord or Rock around 1600 requires contemporary settlement sequences. [Source](https://penetanguishene.ca/media/zwqkxsa1/2-stage-1-and-2-archaeological-assessment-august-9-2022-138-rse.pdf)
- **Northern and western margins · unresolved divisions**. Nbisiing and Nitaskinan now have homeland cores. The family territories within Nitaskinan, the Erie constituent polities and the inland divide between Wenro and Erie remain unresolved. [Source](https://chautauquacountyny.gov/sites/default/files/inline-files/erie_studies_vol10_no2_012.pdf)
- **Central Pennsylvania · dated land use**. Archaeology places earlier Susquehannock settlements on the North Branch and later concentration in Lancaster County. Assigning the entire intervening interior to the southern polity in 1600 would merge distinct settlement periods. [Source](https://www.millersville.edu/sa/land-acknowledgment/native-summary.php)
- **Vermont and New Hampshire interior**. The NPS landscape history identifies Missisquoi, Sokoki, Pigwacket, Pennacook and Cowasuck communities and seasonal camps. It leaves the interfluves between their named settlement districts unresolved at 1600. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/mabi/mansion_grounds_clr1.pdf)
- **Maine coast between Saco and Penobscot**. Onemechin and Marchin met visitors at Saco in 1606. The account establishes their alliance negotiations but does not identify which coastal stretches each governed. The transition toward the Kennebec and Bessabes remains unresolved. [Source](https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/467?revision_id=35781)
- **Ottawa Valley interior**. The early Ottawa Valley synthesis distinguishes Kotakoutouemi on the Coulonge and Dumoine. Overlap with later Wolf Lake lands and inland family boundaries in 1600 remain unresolved. [Source](https://www.ubcpress.ca/asset/9468/1/9780774822879.pdf)
- **Lower Hudson and Raritan Bay**. Bergen County Historical Society reconstructs Hackensack lowlands. It describes displacement of Sanhicans and movement of Wisquaskecks to the Raritan after 1628. The earlier inland divide and tenure of Staten Island cannot be taken from the later distribution. [Source](https://www.bergencountyhistory.org/nativeamericans-in-bergen-county)
- **Lower Delaware, Schuylkill and inland Susquehanna divide**. The Delaware archaeological survey locates Shackamaxon, Passayunk, Okehocking, Minguannan, Narraticon and neighboring communities. It does not delimit each community inland. The survey distinguishes Lancaster-area Susquehannock settlements from later expansion of their trade into Delmarva. [Source](https://deldot.gov/environmental/archaeology/sr1/pdf/179/series_179_1_intro.pdf)
- **Upper Eastern Shore: Tockwogh and Wicomiss**. The NPS study locates Tockwogh on the southern Sassafras bank. It considers Indiantown Farm a candidate seat for Wicomiss. Maryland also reports the Rock Hall area. Neither identifies a territorial divide between those communities. The study watershed boundary defines its research area. [Source](https://www.nps.gov/cajo/learn/upload/CAJO_FINAL-ICL-REPORT_2023_0314-508.pdf)
- **Lower St. Lawrence south shore and Témiscouata portages**. The Wolastoqiyik Wahsipekuk describe ancestral land from the Etchemin to the Métis and south into Maine. Secoudon is independently documented in the contact period, but his community cannot be equated with every local landholding. See the adjacent Innu research note for the competing historical attribution. [Source](https://wolastoqiyikwahsipekuk.ca/en/territory)
- **Rivière-du-Loup to Matane**. The federal historical assessment attributes this south-shore reach to Innu use between 1550 and 1652. The Wolastoqiyik Wahsipekuk history also includes this reach. The sources use different scales and give no local boundary between named political communities around 1600. [Source](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2018/eccc/CW66-541-2018-eng.pdf)
- **Montréal and Québec City reaches**. Cartier encountered the St. Lawrence Iroquoian settlements of Hochelaga and Stadacona in the 1530s. Their political continuity to 1600 is unestablished in archaeology. Their sixteenth-century fields do not supply the later boundaries of Wendat, Innu, Algonquin or Haudenosaunee communities. [Source](https://www.archeoquebec.com/en/archaeology-quebec/thematic-files/prehistoric-quebec)
- **Tadoussac and the St. Lawrence trade corridor**. Anadabijou and Innu, Algonquin and Wolastoqiyik allies gathered at the 1603 alliance. The gathering identifies diplomatic and trading use. Which local community governed each bank between the meeting place and upstream settlements remains unresolved. [Source](https://secondaryhistory.learnquebec.ca/Origins-1608/colonization-attempts-and-1603-alliance)
- **Chemung and upper Susquehanna confluence**. Carantouan is the principal community in a three-town polity recorded on the 1615 journey. The Spanish Hill identification near Athens is a proposed location. The positions of its other towns, the alliance relationship around 1600, and its boundary toward the Five Nations remain unresolved. [Source](https://www.lancasterhistory.org/images/stories/JournalArticles/vol17no1pp5_36_862536.pdf)
- **Great Lakes: Adirondack interior and Champlain corridor**. The 1609 expedition documents armed passage through the lake corridor by Champlain and his Indigenous allies against Haudenosaunee opponents. Mohawk and northern allied participation in the encounter does not delineate exclusive mountain hunting districts in 1600. [Source](https://home.nps.gov/articles/adirondacks-europeans-and-american-colonists.htm)
- **Chesapeake and upper Potomac: Shenandoah approaches**. The National Park Service upper Potomac study compares Luray and Monongahela archaeological traditions and the later Massawomeck trading accounts. Locating the upriver trading partners of Henry Fleet does not identify an exclusive circa-1600 frontier with Mannahoac communities east of the ridge. [Source](https://irma.nps.gov/DataStore/FileSource/Get/2266741?filename=CHOH+vol1-Final_upper+segment_May2011.pdf)
- **Western interior: Youghiogheny and Monongahela valleys**. The Pennsylvania park history places Monongahela villages on the Youghiogheny. The name identifies an archaeological tradition. Which named government held the intervening uplands when the villagers dispersed remains unresolved. [Source](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dcnr/recreation/where-to-go/state-parks/find-a-park/ohiopyle-state-park/history)
- **Southern Adirondacks and Lake Champlain**. The project cultural resources study identifies contested Mohawk hunting in portions of the southern Adirondacks and the lower Champlain valley. Western Abenaki communities occupied the eastern shore. Specific hunting margins and exclusive political jurisdiction in the interior are unresolved. [Source](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=%7BD7D0DAD5-082C-4475-86F6-B211DB2E276B%7D)
- **Maryland Piedmont: Monocacy and Patapsco**. The Maryland archaeological synthesis records contact-period settlements along the Monocacy, Potomac and Patapsco and a route across the Blue Ridge. Travel evidence and later Piscataway and Susquehannock occupation leave the 1600 political affiliation of individual upland settlements open. [Source](https://roads.maryland.gov/OED/Native_American_Trails.pdf)
- **Atlantic Delmarva: Assateague and Pocomoke member towns**. Colonial accounts document Annemessex and Manokin within a changing Pocomoke paramountcy. The extent of each member before that consolidation, the Assateague inland frontier, and jurisdiction over the barrier islands remain unresolved. [Source](https://roads.maryland.gov/OED/Native_American_Trails.pdf)
- **Upper Potomac and Allegheny Plateau: Massawomeck homeland hypotheses**. Smith encountered Massawomeck voyagers in 1608. The Maryland State Archives places later villages in the Garrett County area and West Virginia, while the archaeological review finds the homeland uncertain. The identification of multiple archaeological complexes as one confederation is a hypothesis. Neither the 1608 encounter nor the later villages establish control throughout western Pennsylvania. [Source](https://nysarchaeology.org/download/nysaa/bulletin/number_105.pdf)
- **Richelieu and Saint-Francois transitional country**. The W8banaki Nation identifies ancestral Ndakina in Quebec. Its present fishing-code map includes the Richelieu, Lac Saint-Pierre and south-bank rivers. It does not date the jurisdiction of a particular local community along that entire boundary to circa 1600. The northern reconstructed inland core therefore ends in the uplands. [Source](https://gcnwa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/CODE-de-PECHE-W8BANAKI-2025.pdf)
- **Great Lakes: Wenro, Neutral and Erie interface**. Wenro lay east of the Neutral. Archaeologists disagree over whether particular western New York sites belonged to the Wenro, Erie or Neutral. Identifying the Cuba oil spring does not establish the community’s political limits or settle the competing placements around 1600. [Source](https://ontarioarchaeology.org/wp-content/uploads/oa097_2017.pdf)
